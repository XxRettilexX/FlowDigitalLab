Many branding projects start in the wrong place: colours and fonts get chosen before anyone has defined who the brand is actually talking to, and why.

## Positioning comes before aesthetics

A coherent visual identity comes from a precise decision: what makes this company different from others in its space? If the answer is vague, every design choice that follows will be arbitrary.

## One distinctive trait, not ten

Memorable brands lean on one recognisable element — a colour, a shape, a tone of voice — and repeat it with discipline, instead of trying to communicate everything at once.

## The palette follows function, not fashion

A palette isn't "nice" in the abstract — it has to work in the real contexts where the brand lives: a product label, an interface, a billboard. The best test is seeing it applied, not isolated on a screen.

## Guidelines exist for people who didn't write them

A brand guideline only makes sense if someone who wasn't part of the project can apply it correctly without asking questions. If a phone call is needed to understand how to use the logo, the guide hasn't done its job.

Settling these points before opening any design software saves weeks of revisions and, more importantly, avoids ending up with an identity that looks nice but says nothing specific.
