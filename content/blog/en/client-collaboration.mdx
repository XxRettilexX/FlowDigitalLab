Across enough projects, a pattern repeats: the quality of the outcome depends more on collaboration than on the available budget.

## One point of contact, not a committee

When feedback comes from different people with different priorities, the project slows down and decisions start contradicting each other. A single point of contact — even if they consult the rest of the team internally — speeds everything up.

## Specific feedback, not vague impressions

"I'm not convinced" isn't actionable feedback. "The headline is too long to read in two seconds" is. The more concrete the feedback, the fewer revision rounds are needed.

## Response time matters as much as the quote

A project with fast turnarounds (24-48 hours) stays on schedule. A project where feedback arrives after two weeks ends up costing more for both sides, even for the same amount of billed work.

## Revisions are for refining, not starting over

Changing direction after a phase has been approved naturally comes with extra cost and time — that's not the agency being difficult, it's the consequence of work already done based on what was agreed.

Structuring collaboration this way isn't "bureaucratic" — it's simply the fastest way to get to something that actually works.
