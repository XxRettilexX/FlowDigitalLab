Before pushing a site live, it's worth running through a short but strict checklist. You don't need a two-week audit — a handful of systematic checks is enough to avoid the most common surprises.

## 1. Real performance, not just a score

A high score in an automated tool doesn't guarantee a smooth experience on a slow mobile connection. Test the site on a simulated 3G network and a mid-range device, not just on your laptop with fibre.

## 2. Contrast and keyboard navigation

Check that every piece of text meets at least a 4.5:1 contrast ratio, and that the whole site can be navigated with `Tab` without ever getting trapped inside a component.

## 3. No silent layout shift

Images without explicit dimensions, late-loading fonts, banners that appear after render: these are the most common causes of a poor CLS score, and of a site that feels "unstable" to use.

## 4. Basic technical SEO

- Unique title and meta description per page
- A single `h1` per page, with a coherent heading hierarchy
- An up-to-date sitemap and `robots.txt`
- Correct canonical URLs

## 5. Forms tested end to end

It's not enough for a form to "look right" — you need to actually submit it, check that the email arrives, that errors are clear, and that the loading state is visible.

A checklist like this doesn't replace a real QA process, but it drastically reduces the risk of launching a site with obvious problems from day one.
